## Risks

**US-14-01, Promotion engine.** Section 7 row 3 names only stacking and priority. Automatic promotions with no code, the brand, customer group and first order conditions, start date scheduling and the maximum discount cap are all contracted at PROMO-01 to PROMO-19 and are named in no section 7 row, so the engine is larger than section 7 shows.

**US-14-04, Promotion stacking is explicit.** P-003 found no priority and no stacking field anywhere in the coupon schema, and two percentage coupons applied together for a combined 30 percent with no objection. individual_use is the only exclusivity control and it is all or nothing rather than a priority, so Woo contributes nothing to this story except the stacking that has to be stopped.

**US-14-05, Price snapshot.** The price half is proved native: P-002 placed an order for 3 units at 100, raised the product price to 250, re-read the order and got 300 on both the order total and the line total. AC 1 also requires the discount applied to be stored on the line, and once US-14-01 introduces promotions that are not Woo coupons there is nothing native that itemises them onto the order.

**US-14-06, Refund integrity.** AC 1 and AC 2 are proved native by P-014: a 30 partial refund against a 100 order was accepted, a further 200 against 70 remaining was rejected as an invalid refund amount, and the cumulative ceiling held. AC 3 also requires the refused attempt to be logged, which is not native and lands on the audit log at section 7 row 14, so the story is extend rather than native.

**US-14-07, Stock returns on cancellation.** No probe tested restore on cancel or restore on failed payment, so native rests on Technical Design section 6 alone. P-009 found the stock write does not refuse to go negative, two sequential decreases of 1 from a stock of 1 leaving minus 1, so a restore after an oversold decrement returns a wrong quantity rather than a correct one.
