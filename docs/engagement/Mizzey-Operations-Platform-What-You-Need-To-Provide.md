## 1. Why this document exists

The store cannot be finished without the items in this document. Every one of them is something only you can supply.

This is not a formality. On projects of this size, the schedule is almost never lost to development speed. It is lost to waiting: for an account approval, for a design, for the Arabic version of the product descriptions. **The timeline in your Statement of Work begins when the items marked as blocking here are available, not on the day the contract is signed.**

The document is written as a working checklist. Print it, put a name and a date against each row, and it becomes the thing you run your side of the project from.

> **The two that catch everyone**
>
> **The payment merchant account.** It takes longer to approve than anything else on this list, and nothing about it can be accelerated from this side. Start it first, today if possible, before any other item here.
>
> **The Arabic content.** A bilingual store needs two versions of everything a customer reads. The multilingual system, the language switcher and the right to left layouts are built for you. **The words are yours to supply, in English and in Arabic.** Plan the translation alongside the build, not at the end of it.

### 4.1 Image rights, in more detail

You sell products from other brands. Some of the images and descriptions on your marketplace listings were created by those brands, or by the marketplace itself. Moving them to your own store is a different use of that material.

Written confirmation that you hold the rights is required, because it cannot be verified for you. If the rights are not clear for part of the catalogue, it is far better to know that in stage 2 than to discover it the week before launch, when the only options left are bad ones.

### 4.2 Legal text, in more detail

The policies are implemented, not drafted. The system enforces the return window you set, displays the shipping terms you write, and captures the consent your privacy policy describes. The wording of those policies is yours, and should be right for your business and your obligations.

|---|---|---|

| A named reviewer | The person who actually looks at each stage on staging | At kickoff |

| Availability for acceptance testing | Your team runs the acceptance script, with support | Stage 6 |
| Availability for the training session | The handover session, plus the written Arabic manual | Stage 6 |

**Feedback within 5 working days is a contractual term, not a request.** If nothing is received in that period, the item is treated as approved and the project continues. If you need longer, ask before the period ends and you will be given it. The rule exists so the project cannot stall in silence, not to rush your review.

## 7. What you get in return

So that this reads as an exchange rather than a list of demands, here is the other side of it.

* A staging environment available to you throughout, not only at review points
* A weekly written note: what was completed, what is next, what is waiting on you
* A working demonstration at every stage, not a document describing one
* The technical design, site structure and user flows, and the interface designs, all approved by you before anything is built
* Every requirement written down with a reference number, so scope is never a matter of memory
* All Mizzey-specific code delivered to a repository you own, as it is written
* Full documentation, an Arabic administrator manual, training and a technical handover
* 60 calendar days of warranty from go-live
